Export Process, Maintenance, Chemical Cleaning & Rapid Repair FAQs

Q1: Can SOL RUBBER flooring absorb projectile impacts in shooting ranges to eliminate ricochet risks?

A1: Yes. SOL RUBBER manufactures specialized ballistic rubber products engineered from high-density elastomeric compounds. The dense matrix encapsulates projectile cores, absorbing kinetic energy and eliminating ricochets and lead splatter hazards. Custom formulations are available upon request.

Q2: What pre-shipment inspection steps does factory QC perform to ensure zero dispatch errors?

A2: Inspectors verify delivered goods against the contract item by item: thickness, dimensions, surface texture, and custom colors. Pallets are tallied against shipping packing lists to prevent short-shipments, over-shipments, or mixing unapproved SKUs.

Q3: What is the standard commercial delivery and export process for foreign trade orders?

A3: 1. Finalize product specs, quantities, and colors. 2. Production starts upon receipt of 30% T/T advance deposit. 3. Ocean container booking upon bulk completion (default ocean FCL, or buyer-appointed logistics). 4. Cargo dispatched to Qingdao Port; balance settled against Bill of Lading copy.

Q4: What is the standard factory production lead time for a 20-foot container order?

A4: Standard production lead time for a standard 20-foot container (20'FCL) bulk order is 15 to 20 calendar days from the date the contract deposit is confirmed to cargo readiness.

Q5: What comprehensive after-sales warranty and service support does SOL RUBBER provide?

A5: Service spans the full project lifecycle: pre-sales technical selection, free samples, price quotes, shipping booking, and on-site installation guidance. In the event of non-human cracking, sinkage, or crumb shedding within the warranty term, 100% free replacement tiles or merchandise credits are issued.

Q6: Which types of cleaning equipment are approved for SOL RUBBER flooring, and can scrubbers and vacuums be used directly?

A6: Vacuums with soft brushes can be used directly; auto-scrubbers must be adjusted to specific operating parameters (soft nylon brushes or white polishing pads, neutral cleaner, low-speed scrub, and immediate vacuum water extraction). Prohibited: wire-brush polishers and steam machines exceeding 60°C.

Q7: What are the recommended maintenance frequencies for low, medium-high, and ultra-high traffic facilities?

A7: Low traffic (home/studio): daily dry electrostatic dust mop, weekly damp mop with neutral cleaner, monthly low-speed buffer. Medium-high (commercial gym): 3x daily dry dust mop, 1–2x weekly full damp mop with neutral cleaner, quarterly deep scrub and wax. Ultra-high (arenas/terminals): dry mop every 2 hours, 2–3x weekly damp scrub.

Q8: What chemical cleaner restrictions and cleaning precautions must be observed for SOL RUBBER flooring?

A8: Use exclusively neutral-pH cleaners (pH 6–8); strictly prohibit strong acids, strong alkalis, acetone, turpentine, and soap containing copper or manganese. For gum or wax: freeze with plastic-wrapped ice and scrape with plastic putty knife. For chocolate or grease: soak with neutral cleaner for 3–5 minutes and scrub with a soft brush.

Q9: What visual and physical diagnostic signals indicate that rubber flooring has been damaged by chemical cleaners?

A9: Visual: Localized white haze, dull patchy discoloration (acid/alkali etch), surface blistering/swelling, edge curling (solvent plasticizer dissolution), or fine web-like cracking. Physical: Brittleness, white crease marks when flexed (polymer degradation), loss of wet slip resistance (micro-pores eroded smooth), or surface stickiness.

Q10: What is the 4-level remediation protocol for repairing rubber flooring damaged by chemical cleaners?

A10: Level 1 (Emergency): Neutralize acid with baking soda solution; rinse alkali/solvent with clean water and dry. Level 2 (Light haze): Wet-sand with 2000–3000 grit paper, apply polish compound, and wax (cure 24h). Level 3 (Medium peeling/cracking): Scrape loose layer, apply color-matched filler paste in layers, sand flat, and wax. Level 4 (Severe degradation): Replace damaged tiles (72h cure).

Q11: Which cleaning solutions cause zero damage to SOL RUBBER flooring during daily maintenance?

A11: Zero-damage options are dedicated neutral rubber floor cleaners strictly controlled within pH 6–8, or warm water (approx. 30°C) mixed with diluted mild neutral dishwashing liquid or laundry detergent. Prohibited: bleach, oxalic acid, descalers (pH < 5 or > 9), gasoline, kerosene, turpentine, acetone, and WD-40.

Q12: What is the rapid no-demolition injection repair procedure for localized hollow bubbling under rubber tiles?

A12: A 15–30 minute procedure (walkable in 2 hours): 1. Clean boundary seams and vacuum dust. 2. Draw specialized flexible polyurethane adhesive into a syringe and inject slowly into the hollow cavity until slight squeeze-out occurs at seams. 3. Tap flat with a rubber mallet, place heavy steel weights, and wipe excess glue with alcohol. Allow 2 hours set, 24 hours full cure.

Q13: How can loosened interlocking puzzle joints be reinforced and locked without demolishing the floor?

A13: A 30-minute non-destructive procedure (walkable in 2 hours): 1. Clean accumulated dust from loosened puzzle tabs. 2. Use a plastic pry bar and rubber mallet to re-engage tabs flush. 3. Apply a small amount of flexible locking adhesive to hidden tab edges (do not fill completely; preserve expansion gaps). 4. Inset color-matched rubber filler strip into open seam and mask with tape for 2 hours.

Q14: What standard toolkit and 5-step SOP are utilized for rapid repair of debonded rubber flooring rolls?

A14: Toolkit enables 15-minute repair (1-hour cure): 1. Subfloor prep: Lift debonded roll edge with plastic spatula, clean dust, and mask borders. 2. Moisture check: Ensure concrete moisture ≤ 8%. 3. Glue injection: Inject specialized flexible PU adhesive and spread evenly under cavity. 4. Rolling: Roll from center outward using steel roller to expel air, then place weights for 1 hour. 5. Clean: Wipe squeeze-out with alcohol swabs.

Q15: What is the detailed 3-layer material configuration of SOL RUBBER composite gym flooring?

A15: 1. Bottom core (≥90% of total thickness): High-density recycled SBR tire rubber granules vulcanized under high heat for core shock and impact noise absorption. 2. Surface wear layer: High-purity colored EPDM granules heat-vulcanized with the core (friction coefficient ≥ 0.6, UV-stable). 3. Intermediate bonding: Solvent-free MDI polyurethane binder cross-linked throughout, free of lead and phthalates.
